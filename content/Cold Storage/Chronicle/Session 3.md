# Session 3 — Power Prey
Stream link: [https://youtube.com/live/IiMd9ehnhKE](https://youtube.com/live/IiMd9ehnhKE)

## Session Summary

[[Mitch]] arrived at the Darkhour gate having just been nearly run down by the Charger — eyes locked on the wide-eyed driver as she blew past him at twice the speed limit, no swerve, no recognition, just a woman fleeing a scene. Up on the second floor, [[Spider]] was sitting against a wall, blood drying on his face and clothes, [[Steve Jensen]] cooling on the floor between him and [[Trip]]. Whatever instinct had carried him up the stairs had let him out at the bottom. He sat with what he had done and worked his way back into the body that contained him.

The coterie consolidated and worked the room. The unconscious gate runner — the one who knee'd [[Claire]] in the temple — had nothing on him: a collapsible baton and the deliberate absence of anything else. No wallet, no ID, no badge, no marker of any kind. Someone had walked him into the night sterile. [[Claire]] read his blood as fleeting Melancholic and took two dots of hunger off him, clean against her Convictions and clean against the Hunt — a man who stalked [[Carter]] for pay was not someone she owed restraint.

He woke up calling himself **Jim** (later, briefly, Clarence and then Lance and then back to Jim). Darkhour took odd jobs — security work, PI work if the money was right. They had been hired for tonight by an anonymous client called **[[Redwood]]**. [[Steve Jensen|Jensen]] was the only one who had ever spoken with Redwood face to face. The building they were standing in had been provided by Redwood roughly two weeks ago — _the guy bought us the building_ — confirmed by a printed email in the room directing them into the space. Jim hadn't done the calls. He'd taken the pictures. He kept saying _a couple of love taps_ to describe what had been done to [[Carter]], and [[Mitch]], reading him through the scalpel he was not yet using, realized that this man genuinely did not know that _love taps_ meant a hospitalization. A line in one of the printed emails confirmed it: the team had been told explicitly not to lay hands on the targets. Jensen had freelanced. Jim had been in the car.

[[Mitch]] put the scalpel away and put Jim back in the chair.

While the coterie was deciding what to do with him, [[Mitch]] worked the wall — and under one of the older photos of [[Carter]], pinned beneath the picture, found a sticky note. **The blackmail list.** [[Carter]] Marshall. **Raphael Marte**. **Megan** (whom [[Trip]] recognized from his own situation). **Gladys Ackerman**. **[[Jonathan]]** — the name [[Collin]] recognized. The list resolved a structural question that had been hanging since the start: every coterie member had been blackmailed _because someone they loved was on this list_. [[Collin]] had not been approached because [[Jonathan]] had not yet been taken. The night [[Walter Nash]] called in his favors and [[Collin]] showed up unsolicited to investigate, his office had not been set up for him for the first time in his standing arrangement. He stepped outside, called [[Jonathan]], got voicemail. [[Trip]] called Megan — anxious, alive, safe. [[Claire]] called Raphael — no change in his situation. [[Trip]] offered to track [[Jonathan]] by Blood Sorcery if they could get to a place he could work.

The plan to call [[Redwood]] from [[Jensen]]'s burner went forward anyway. They had Jim play it — panicked, winded, _Jensen's down, the targets got away, what do I do._ Jim sold it, drawing on his middle-school _Pirates of Penzance_ run as a tree. The number was no longer in service. [[Redwood]] had burned the line the moment the op went silent. Single-burner-per-op tradecraft, professional, and confirmation that whatever Redwood was, he was not improvising.

They let Jim go. [[Collin]] put a pistol against his temple and made the cost of return clear. [[Trip]] handed him pills _one a night, no more_ — Jim dropped them in the first bush past the gate and disappeared. [[Steve Jensen|Jensen]]'s body went into the trunk. [[Collin]] would call in a John Doe later. They gathered everything else from the office — the photos, the sticky notes, the printed emails, the laptop — and drove to [[Collin]]'s shop.

[[Collin]]'s gunsmithing space, in the gang district behind a roll-up door, gave the coterie its first quiet hour of the night. [[Trip]] began the ritual prep — bloody gauze, scalpel, vial — while [[Collin]] went into the back to scrub his surveillance footage. The feed showed [[Jonathan]] handling a transaction with familiar customers, taking [[Collin]]'s call about online research, opening his laptop, then visibly stopping himself: _I'm not this stupid. I have to get off this IP._ He'd closed the laptop, taken it with him, locked up, and walked out under his own power. Wherever he was now, he had walked there before he'd been taken.

In the bay, [[Spider]] sat with what he had done. [[Trip]], hands occupied with the ritual, asked questions. [[Spider]] said it had been years since his hunger had been quiet, and that [[Steve Jensen|Jensen]] had been the first person he had killed in feeding. [[Mitch]], who hadn't yet told the others what had happened at the clinic, told them. _It changed me, and I thought I had it under wraps, and then I lost it on Trip's contact._ [[Spider]] said _I got your back_ and meant it. The three monsters in the bay recognized each other.

[[Trip]] burned the gauze. The trail manifested as a faint red mist heading for the door, then dissipated in the wind as he stepped through it. The ritual roll had succeeded; the survival check that would have let him follow it had not.

That was when [[Collin]]'s phone rang.

A distorted voice on an unknown number: _I have your special friend with me. While you've been busy, I've been working too. You take something of mine, I take something of yours. I know you're on your way. Time is running out. Tick tock._ And cut the line.

[[Collin]] gave in to the fury frenzy and rode it. He destroyed the phone first, hammering it against the corner of his workbench. [[Spider]] and [[Mitch]] grappled him together — five successes between them, [[Spider]]'s arms iron — and held him in place while [[Claire]] put her face in his and refused to let him fall. _Get your shit together. We won't find them if you lose your shit. We need your shit._ The frenzy broke. The room held him. [[Mitch]] and [[Spider]] each took a moment of being the people who pulled someone else back from the edge they had each crossed earlier that night.

[[Trip]], hungry now and getting hungrier, paid the Rouse he hadn't wanted to pay and tried the trail again. The mist resolved into a painted neon line in the air. _Get in the car._

The trail led them to **[[Garfield Park]]** — and to a recognition that [[Trip]] and [[Spider]] both arrived at: [[Garfield Park]] Conservatory was an Elysium. _Clean up your mess, but all are free to feed._ The coterie sat in that grace for a moment. [[Trip]] approached a city bylaw officer named **Williams**, dominated him into testing what he claimed were sugar pills, took three dots, set him on a bench, called _assistance needed_ on the man's own radio, and walked back to the car. [[Claire]], who had argued against feeding on someone unhoused before the Elysium status was confirmed and who had not retracted the argument once it was, watched without a word and did not let her Heightened Senses lapse. The trail came in to a bench and went out a different way. [[Jonathan]] had been moved.

It led them, finally, to a residential cul-de-sac and an eight-foot chain-link fence around a two-story home that did not match its neighborhood. Marble statues of Greek and Roman myth scenes scattered across the yard. A large statue of Hercules at the front door — the **Hannah-Barbera Hercules**, with the **H** on his belt — placed at the entrance like a warning to anyone who would understand. A bicycle on its side. A football near a koi pond. Sun-bleached chairs with a magazine on one of them. A flawless paint job on red brick. Lights in two second-floor windows, blinds drawn, shadows occasionally moving through them. From the upper floor: a female voice, exhausted and annoyed; a male voice, insistent, shutting down whatever conversation was being had. Then _Hide. Go._

[[Collin]] scanned the perimeter — no visible cameras, an intercom on the gate. [[Spider]] paid a Rouse and went up the wall, climbing brick like an insect on a windowpane, looking for a gap in the blinds he wouldn't find. [[Mitch]] reached for the fence and was thrown back by the shock — and the dogs came. Two German shepherds, larger and faster than they should have been, hit the inside of the fence at full pace and stopped only because they knew not to touch it.

The intercom crackled.

> _Tell me this, you blood-sucking bastards. How many pints can a 110-pound 15-year-old girl lose before she becomes irrevocably brain-damaged?_

[[Trip]], whose Academics specialty was human anatomy, stepped to the box. _Somewhere between three and five. It depends._ The voice on the other side said one word in Dutch. The dogs went silent and lay down. The gate clicked open.

[[Mitch]], whose Animal Ken caught what the others couldn't — too big, too fast, almost wolf-sized — grabbed [[Trip]] before he could step through.

_Hold up. Those aren't dogs._

End of session. The coterie reconvenes tomorrow night to walk through the gate.
