# Session 5 — The Sacrifice (Night One)

> **The Sacrifice — Story 2 — opens.** A few months on from "Power Prey," the framing shifts from personal to political. The coterie is now an official watchman crew for the Chicago Camarilla; tonight the Prince hands them a delegation to guard and three troublemakers to quiet — subtly, no stains on the ground.

## Session Summary

Some months have passed since the [[Hercules House]] killbox — *early-mid winter then; late summer now, of the following year.* (A virus out of China is not yet rumor, but it isn't far off.) [[Mitch]] and [[Spider]] both held onto themselves in the aftermath of the kills they made that night — remorse checks passed, Spider's a critical — *able to separate the self from the isolated act, but drawing no closer to the Beast.* In the interim the coterie has been made, whether they wanted it or not, an **official Chicago Camarilla coterie of the watchman stripe**: eyes and ears on threats to the city's Kindred — anarchs, hunters, Sabbat, werewolves. Anarchs and hunters they've had to head off already. The rarer horrors they wake each night hoping stay rare.

[[Mitch]] and [[Spider]] now share a haven — a neglected **theater** with a green-space facade, a back-alley postern, and a manhole a few steps from the door (Spider's investment, [[Collin]]'s security rig, a small army of Nosferatu hands). [[Claire Evangelista Marte|Claire]], [[Trip]], and [[Collin]] keep their own. The night opens with an index card slid under each of their doors in the same hand: an address for **Otali's**, a fast-food franchise, the word *right now*, and a signature — **[[Damian]]**, the Sheriff of the Chicago Camarilla.

At Otali's, Damian sits with an untouched burger and a manila envelope, *uncharacteristically nervous* — leg tapping, posture too vertical, here because he was told to be, not because he wanted to be. He centers the envelope, nods, and leaves. The coterie reads its contents in the car: **the Prince's letter**, written in code — **SC** is the **Succubus Club** (Elysium), the *social club* is Kindred society, the *elite club* the Camarilla, *my family* Clan Ventrue. The job: a two-Kindred **delegation** arrives at **O'Hare Terminal 5** tomorrow night, to be met outside **Herrick's Grocers** in arrivals at **22:00**, held somewhere safe overnight, and delivered to the **third floor of the Succubus Club at 21:00 the night after — intact and well-rested.** A car waits in lot zone **H22** (keys and registration enclosed). Also in the envelope: **four one-use flip phones**, each programmed to a single number — *not the Prince, but someone who can help them succeed if needed.* And three dossiers on three troublemakers to subdue, quietly, *before the fire begins* — **no stains on the ground**, lest the act itself hand the anarchs the grievance the Prince fears.

The delegation reads as *important*: two Kindred, a small-framed younger East Asian woman with long dark hair and a bald, bearded "unkempt" older white man. Nobody travels as cargo by daylight and survives an unscheduled landing, so the pair will arrive *shipped* in some fashion and walk out through arrivals — the coterie's only real job at the airport is to make sure nothing redirects them through security.

**The three names.** *Fly Boy* — **[[Noah Grewal]]**, who claims O'Hare and leans on **[[Felicity Miller]]** of the Department of Aviation's executive team; the read is that neutralizing *her* (not him) breaks his ability to flag the guests. *Crook* — **[[Charles Dawson]]**, of whom even Spider's occult knowledge turns up nothing. *[[Gengis]]* — an anarch of real reputation, rumored within Tremere circles to be *a warlock's pawn, mind seeded with commands that turn him against anarch interests*; Collin has heard both [[Damian]] and **Critias** (Brujah Primogen) would like him dead. The dossier's mention of *Tyler* — *"followed in Tyler's footsteps"* — points to a famous Brujah elder, an anarch legend who once held absolute control of Chicago's airport in the '80s before relocating to New York. The coterie splits the labor: [[Collin]] burns a contact for intel on Crook; [[Trip]] calls his sire for a line on Gengis; [[Spider]] pulls flight schedules (no private aircraft logged in the arrival window — as expected for guests who travel hidden).

Before fieldwork, Trip spends twenty minutes and a measure of his own blood on **Sanguine Watcher**, conjuring a spy-rat with a faint red sheen to its eyes. He names it **Lethe** — *like the river.* A living camera on four legs, good for the night.

**Crooks Lounge, Rogers Park.** The dossier's lead is a closed-for-renovation bar — and Collin's people confirm the name isn't a coincidence: this is the *original* **[[Crooks Lounge]]**, the franchise's first location, founded by a man called Crook (the location where the coterie once met [[Walter Nash]] was a *different* Crooks Lounge). Lethe slips a vent and Trip, watching through its eyes, catches a glimpse of a **Nosferatu hunched over a limp blonde man, fangs in his throat.** Spider creeps to the back to boost the rat through a higher vent — and finds the back door cracked, a Nosferatu watching him from the shadows. Game recognizes game: the reception is *hospitable*, not hostile — *"What do you need? Something to eat, a place to sleep?"* This is **Crook himself** — Camarilla, not anarch, a face Spider half-remembers from a room years ago. A collector of favors. *"If he smiles, someone's losing money."*

The coterie comes in. Crook is bored, ancient, and motivated by *fun*, not power or reprisal — and he quickly works out what the dossier's authors didn't say: the delegation is *important*, and the coterie was chosen precisely *because nobody knows who they are.* The idea of making the guests' patron have a bad night strikes him as *really funny.* The negotiation goes sideways — a messy critical lands a **paranoia compulsion** on Trip mid-pitch — but the coterie talks their way to terms. **The deal:** if the delegation reaches its destination *without Crook or his people being seen,* the coterie owes him **five minor boons — one each.** If they *are* seen, the ledger's clean. He shakes on it with a great polite smile. *A chance he leaves them alone; the same as a chance.* Read of his haven: stereotypically Nosferatu but *simple* — Crook is not an especially old Kindred, and the feeding scene they watched left no trace by the time they walked in.

**Alfred's penthouse.** [[Trip]] brings the coterie to his sire, **[[Alfred Ratimir]]** — old-Chicago Tremere, photographer, Godfather-by-way-of-Pacino, the coterie's **Mawla**. The suite is the whole floor, seventy-odd stories up; he greets them *"Enter freely and of your own free will"* (the Dracula line, with a knowing grin) and offers blood from a *super-compelled* white-coated servant who slices his own hand to fill the glasses. Trip is handed vitae rather than mortal blood — and politely declines to drink it. The intel reframes everything:

- There is **no Tremere chantry in Chicago** anymore — the Pyramid fell when Vienna burned to the Second Inquisition; only scattered houses since. Chicago has **[[Abraham D'Abler]]**, a rare elder who walked into the Beckoning and *came back* — an old-school Cam loyalist with no political appetite (and the Tremere who first set Trip on [[Walter Nash]]). There is, accordingly, *no real Tremere opposition to the Banu Haqim* in this city.
- The "Gengis is a Tremere pawn" rumor is a **trap**. Gengis is associated with **[[House Carna]]** — a Tremere splinter (feminist, Wiccan, non-traditionalist, vilified by the patriarchal clan), with members on both sides of the Camarilla/anarch divide. Its most important figure is the antitribu **[[Erika]]** — possibly its true head — and Alfred's read is that *she* seeded the pawn rumor to make the arrogant overreach and mishandle her friends. The Tremere angle is a dead end.
- **Gengis** himself is *the* face of the Chicago anarch movement — ugly, pierced, anti-establishment, hiding behind mortal activists; his standing only *strengthened* since Collin's own clan defected to the anarch side and disaffected Brujah began flocking to him. He's *wealthy* — unusual for his kind — so buying him off would mean deep pockets. He's also a *brute*, and currently *not at his penthouse* on the Gold Coast.

As Trip absorbs all this, Alfred quietly works a low-level blood sorcery on him — *spiking his hunger, glass of vitae trembling in his hand* — a paternal power play, *toying, not malice.* Trip holds against a hunger frenzy (barely), excuses himself briskly, and stops at his clinic on the way out to feed off his Herd.

**The Gold Coast.** Gengis's address (**126 E. Cedar**, per the dossier) turns out to be a gated villa community, the Sex Pistols pouring out of one three-storey house at full volume to spite the neighbors — but he isn't home, and the grounds are camera-covered. Collin places remote surveillance from a distance rather than risk being filmed up close. Spider scales in on Celerity and Potence with Lethe; the rat finds nothing at rat height, and Spider — *hunger climbing, frustrated* — slams a fist into the marble kitchen island hard enough to crack it. A hidden compartment pops. Inside: a single matchbox stamped **Mohawk Bar.**

*Mysteries and matchboxes.* The night ends on the new lead — and on a coterie still mid-mission, with Fly Boy's lever untouched, Gengis only located by a thread, and Crook's "boring night" promised but never guaranteed.
