# Session 6 — The Sacrifice (Night One, concluded)

> **The prep night runs to dawn.** Picking up the instant the last one ended, the coterie spends the rest of the first night clearing the board for tomorrow's delegation pickup: a punk dive to read [[Gengis]], an after-midnight walk into O'Hare to handle [[Noah Grewal|Fly Boy]], and a trail of stolen cars that says the anarchs already know what's coming. Three problems, three different prices.

## Session Summary

The night the Prince's letter arrived is still unfolding. Two Kindred are coming to Chicago — no names, no clan, no stated purpose — and everyone who matters, Prince and anarchs and troublemakers alike, is already treating them as *important*. That agreement is the dangerous part: people are choosing sides over guests nobody has seen.

**Outside [[Gengis's Villa]].** [[Spider]] rejoins the others at the gate, the Sex Pistols still pouring out of the house, a **[[Mohawk Bar]]** matchbox in hand and a cracked marble island left behind him. The surveillance sweep of the garage turns up two Harley-Davidsons, a blacked-out late-model Lexus, and an early-2000s orange-yellow Honda S2000 — and *no empty stall*, so wherever Gengis went, he didn't drive. The read keeps not adding up: a self-styled anarch punk living behind a gate in a maid-clean villa. *Probably the front is the personality, not the money.* [[Claire Evangelista Marte|Claire]] reads the deeper picture (Politics + Intelligence, helped by the coterie's anarch contact **Pollock**): Gengis isn't merely one of three names on a dossier — he's an *anarch leader*. Chicago keeps few of them, by the Prince's design, but of those who remain Gengis is well known, and Claire will know his face on sight now.

**The [[Mohawk Bar]].** A grimy British-invasion-punk dive frozen in its own heyday — graffiti, old band posters, a 2.3-star reputation, unfriendly to newcomers, not gang-tied, *not* a known Kindred haunt. The plan is reconnaissance only; nobody intends to act tonight unless something concrete surfaces. [[Trip]]'s rat **Lethe** slips in and finds Gengis up on the mezzanine with a young woman — then a bartender whips a glass at it and it bolts. So the coterie goes in on foot: [[Collin]] and Spider, a couple of minutes apart, **Blush of Life** up (Spider's holds for the whole night), an earpiece between Collin and Claire, Lethe riding in Spider's hood while Claire and Trip wait in the car.

Socially it goes sideways. Collin is clocked as a cop the moment he opens his mouth — conversations die around him. Spider gets a beer "accidentally" tipped down his hoodie and a sneer — *"Did you get this from Hot Topic?"* — and a boo from the back. From the mezzanine, cigar in mouth, Gengis catches Collin's eye and flashes **Eyes of the Beast** — *I know what you are.* Not who. Just Kindred. Collin gives him a small nod; Gengis answers with a smirk that isn't friendly and turns back to his guest.

Spider turns the bust into the job. He ducks into the ladies' room, drops into **Unseen Passage**, and uses **Uncanny Grip** to climb up under the perforated-metal walkway of the mezzanine and hang there to listen (two Rouse checks, Hunger climbing). What he gathers: the woman is mortal — she's telling Gengis about *yesterday's* anti-fascist protest, the one he turned up to *late*, after dark — and Gengis either genuinely cares about that movement or performs it convincingly. A punk comes up the stairs: *"Cars will be ready."* Gengis: *"Great, thanks for letting me know."* First thread: cars. An hour on, after the woman steps away, the downstairs crew comes up and Gengis briefs them — *because that cop didn't even have the balls to talk to me, I don't think it comes to blows tomorrow, but bring the bats just in case* — and tells them to watch him for **who to swing on and who not to**, *"might be a way to make some new friends."* Spider's read lands hard: Gengis isn't guessing. He seems to **know who's arriving tomorrow**, and there's a confrontation already planned around it. (Collin, by then, has already made his exit — Gengis muttered *"coward"* at his back.)

**O'Hare, after midnight.** Rewinding the same hours, Claire, Trip, and Collin drive out to the airport and spend a while overthinking Felicity Miller before the obvious lands: they are vampires, and every wall they keep hitting — appointments, emails, phone trees — is a *mortal* wall that doesn't survive a Compel to someone's face. Claire pulls the public terminal map (a chunk of it just reads **authorized access only**, contents unmarked) and they note something off — guards walking the concourse with **MP7 submachine guns** and heavier body armor than any airport wears on a normal night. Trip Compels the info desk and they're walked back to **Felicity Miller**, the Aviation Department *Commissioner*, still at her desk at 12:07 a.m.; three visitor passes, good for six hours.

She's wary — and Claire, sitting lower, catches what the others miss: Felicity's right hand is on the desk phone with a finger resting on the security-call button. Trip plays a private-clinic doctor *("Dr. Trip Ratimir... shipping out with Doctors Without Borders, questions about transporting meds")*; she doesn't buy a word of it but sends him off to book an appointment like a normal person. The instant the door shuts, her phone rings and Trip listens in (**Heightened Senses**): a man's voice — **Noah** — paranoid, *"Who were they? Did they mention me?"* — and Felicity *annoyed*, brushing him off, *"some Doctors Without Borders nonsense, nothing to do with you, stop being so paranoid."* Three reads fall out of that call: her irritation is **not** a blood-bound thrall's devotion; Noah had to *phone* her, so there's no ghoul's telepathic leash; and Noah is *"keeping an eye on"* them — almost certainly through a ghoul on the airport cameras. Trip also has her face now, which is its own option.

So they double back and do it directly. Trip Compels Felicity to take them to Noah — she fights it (*"why would I... how do you even know him?"*), the command grinding against the fact that she can't actually clear them in herself, but Intimidation + Manipulation settles it: *"Your business is with him. Leave me out of it."* She leads them up through the authorized-only offices — stairs, security checkpoints, three coded doors, glass-walled corridors behind Terminal 2 (one side branch has an unmarked solid **fire door** they don't go to) — to a frosted-glass office at the end. *"That's Noah's. Never saw you."* And she's gone.

**[[Noah Grewal|Fly Boy]].** He sits facing the runway window, back to the room — denying Trip eye contact on purpose (no line for Dominate, and a poker face, and a little theater). White linen suit. He claims O'Hare as his domain; says someone paid up front to ship the *"alternative Kindred"* in **heavy-duty speaker cases**, that he means to learn who's in the boxes, and that he can just as easily lock them back up and put them on the next flight to JFK. Trip presses the weak point: the Prince doesn't recognize the claim, the coterie walked in *easily*, and Noah is sitting here **alone**, no coterie behind him. Noah's opening price is steep — a number worth **two dots of Resources** for the rest of the story. The haggling (a Willpower reroll and a Blood Surge get it to three successes, short of the five they wanted) lands at **one dot** — but Noah tacks on *carte blanche to feed in the coterie's domain for the year*, which the coterie won't give: tithing him a person a week runs straight into their Tenet that *mortal lives are not theirs to spend*. The deal that closes is smaller and truer to what Noah actually wants: **one dot of Resources** (Trip eats the loss) and **first call** the moment they learn *who the delegation is and why they're here.* That's the whole game for him — to be included, to be told yes. He hands over a business card that's nothing but a phone number, and lets the guests through.

**The stolen cars.** Back at the closing-down Mohawk, Trip casts **Illuminate the Trail of Prey** on the punk whose face Lethe captured, and backtracks his last day. Pushing through the reek of booze and vomit outside the bar, the trail clears and runs back past a liquor store and a gas station to an **impound lot** — gate chain cut by bolt cutters. The anarchs **stole vehicles** here for tomorrow; the man Spider watched wasn't riding in them, he was staging them. The coterie burns one of the **four one-use flip phones** from the Prince's letter — a woman's voice answers — to flag the stolen cars and ask for help, and drops an anonymous non-emergency tip to the police on top of it.

**Setting the table.** They settle on holding the delegation at the **[[Theater Haven]]** — safest from Crook once they're inside the domain, the real risk being Gengis hitting them *before* they get there, and the worry of leading an anarch warband to a Prince-granted haven. Plan: pick up at Terminal 5 at 22:00 tomorrow, shell-game the cars, shake Gengis before the theater, slip in the back if it comes to it. **Dutch** is set to grab matching hoodies for the shell game and dress the theater up — two rooms for two day-sleeping guests, movies, the works. On information, the coterie splits: Trip wants **plausible deniability**, to be told nothing about who the guests are; Claire argues for knowing what they safely can and *curating* what gets fed back to Fly Boy. The cars get assigned — Vaughn driving both delegates and Collin (the gun); Dutch driving Spider and Claire; Trip driving himself and Mitch; the Camarilla's car (lot **H22**) burned as a decoy — and Collin readies his **hidden armory** (a second pistol and a long gun, ammunition) as deterrence against bats, hoping not to fire. Whether Gengis's crew are mortal, ghoul, or Kindred stays open, though at least some read as mortal — they were drinking themselves sloppy at the bar.

The night's last beats. The flip-phone ally answers, leaving an envelope shoved up through the rear-alley manhole: *three trucks and two cars taken; trucks neutralized, the two cars not yet found.* Spider and Trip both close the night at Hunger 2 and fix it — Trip off his herd contact **George**, B-positive, which since he gorged on fresh blood at the killbox now tastes like ash he drinks as penance. And when Trip asks Spider for a measure of vitae to prep his room-silencing ritual, Claire smells it — *the bane of her own clan* — and a growl climbs her throat before she shuts it down. Spider sees it clearly for the first time: the rest of them are predators of mortals, and **Claire is a predator of vampires.** Then the door closes over it. *"You're good, and that keeps me good."* She stays at the theater, and lets Raphael know.

Night One is done. Tomorrow the boxes land.
