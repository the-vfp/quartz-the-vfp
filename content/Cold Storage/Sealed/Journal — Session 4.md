It's over. Your name is off the list. [[Jonathan]] is home. The family is safe. You can come home now — all of you.[^r1]

Now the rest. I don't lie to you, not even the parts that would let me sleep better.

I got hurt. One of the dogs opened my throat before [[Mitch]]'s claws and [[Spider]]'s hands put it down — and I mean opened, Raphael, the kind of wound that doesn't close on its own and that I wouldn't take the blood to close. Bad, survivable, mine to carry; you'll picture worse than it is if I leave it vague, so: a torn throat and a night spent slow and half-deaf from the gunfire, and it will mend on its own time because I've decided it will. Don't let the girls see my neck until it's further along.[^r2]

The house was built for us. Not as a figure of speech — a trap, engineered. A fireball rigged into the grandfather clock. Stakes set to swing down the stairwell. Six men in the rafters with suppressed rifles and a patch on every shoulder, a dagger driven through a skull: the [[Night's Cross]], a whole crew of hunters who know what we are and how to end us and do it anyway.

It isn't the guns I keep returning to. It's the walls. With my senses up I could see the clean pale rectangles all over them, where photographs had hung and been taken down. He stripped his own home of every face before we came — not in grief, in operational security; he knew we were coming and would not leave us pictures of his people. I understood the man from those empty rectangles before I ever saw him, and I hated that I understood him.[^r3]

His name is [[Redwood|Paul Sterry]]. *Redwood* was only ever a handle. A mortal — middle-aged, thick glasses, a knife at Jonathan's throat — whose fifteen-year-old daughter, [[Michelle Sterry|Michelle]], was fed on by [[Walter Nash]] and dumped in a park and took more than a month to die. That is the whole engine of it. A father lost his girl to one of us, learned we were real, and spent everything he had building a machine to make us hurt back. The blackmail, the hired hunters, the dogs raised on stolen blood, a starved thing padlocked in the basement — all of it grief, organized.

I reached for his mind and hit a wall; he'd prepared for that too. *Don't try your tricks. I know what you are. You took my Michelle.* What took the knife down wasn't a power and it wasn't a lie. It was [[Trip]], who stopped performing and confessed — named everyone we'd hurt across these two nights, out loud, and told the man he was sorry about his daughter and meant it. The blade dropped.

I wanted him dead, and I won't dress that up. He'd seen our faces, he knew where you are, he'd armed men to put stakes in us, and every cold line of reasoning ended in the same place. And then the next cold line said: use the wife, use the son, trade what's left of his family for standing with the Prince. I balked. I'm a mother, and I had just spent two nights learning exactly what a parent will build out of a dead child, and I could not be the one to do to his family what was done to ours. So we didn't decide it alone. We carried him up the chain.[^r4]

[[Kevin Jackson|The Prince]] kept him alive and useful — he'll be pointed at the Sabbat now, his grief re-aimed — and spared the wife and the boy and sent them somewhere safe. For surviving a hunter ambush as near-strangers to his court, he gave us first claim on a Blood Hunt against [[Walter Nash]], the one who actually killed her. Nash is ours next, if we want him.[^r5]

I keep thinking about Michelle, and about Paul at his window taking down every photograph so no one could use his children against him, and then about you, somewhere I won't write down, doing the same arithmetic from the other side of it. He and I were doing the same math from opposite ends, and the only thing that came out different is that his daughter is already dead and mine are not. That isn't virtue. It's luck, and I'm not going to waste it.

Come home. Bring Holly and Joy. I want my face still and my voice level when I see them, and I'll have the day to practice. Tell them their mother's work trip is over — it's true enough, and the rest is mine to carry.
