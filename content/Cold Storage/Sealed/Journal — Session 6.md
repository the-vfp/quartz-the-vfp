I'm not home tonight. I'm writing from the theater the others keep — I'll wait out the day here before we collect our guests — and the velvet and the dark and the smell of [[Spider]]'s housekeeping are a long way from your hallway, which I notice more than I'd like.

Gengis first, because I had him wrong on paper and right in person. The dossier listed him as one of three nuisances; he isn't. He's an anarch leader — one of the few the Prince allows to keep breathing in this city — and now that I've placed him I'll know his face anywhere, which is the kind of thing I collect and you know it. He wasn't home; he was at a punk bar called the Mohawk, holding court on a mezzanine, and Spider climbed up underneath the walkway and hung there in the dark to listen, which is a sentence I never expected to write about anyone. Spider gathered, from up there, that [[Gengis]] already knows who arrives tomorrow and is building something around it — bring the bats, watch me for who to swing on. The men we were sent to keep in the dark are not in the dark. Someone told him, or he's better than his file, and I don't like either.[^r1]

The airport went better once we stopped overthinking it. We are vampires, and every wall we kept hitting — the appointments, the phone trees, the locked half of the terminal map — was a mortal wall, and mortal walls don't survive [[Trip]] putting his face close to someone's and telling them to comply. We were walked back to [[Felicity Miller]], the Aviation Commissioner, at her desk past midnight, and I sat lower than the others and watched her hands, which is how I caught her finger resting on the security button before she'd decided whether to use it. She's afraid of Fly Boy, not bound to him — he phoned her after we left and she was annoyed, not devoted — so whatever he has on her is leverage or money, the kind that leaves a record, and you'll do more with that than I can.[^r2]

We dealt with [[Noah Grewal|Fly Boy]] directly. He sat facing the runway with his back to the room so Trip couldn't take his eyes — a small, theatrical man in a white suit who claims the whole airport and has no one standing behind him. His opening price was robbery. What he actually wanted was smaller and sadder: to be told. We closed on a measure of Trip's resources and the first call the moment we learn who our guests are and why they've come. He asked for the run of our streets to feed for a year and we wouldn't give it — we don't get to spend a person a week to make a man feel important. So he'll feel included instead.

I'll spare you the rest of the logistics — stolen cars staged for an ambush, a shell game to lose them on the way to the theater, a one-use favor I spent and hope was worth spending.

Near dawn Trip needed a measure of Kindred blood for a silencing rite, and Spider gave it, and the moment it touched the air I smelled it — not blood the way the others smell blood, but the bane of my own clan, the scent of one of us — and a growl was in my throat before I'd decided anything at all. I put it down. It took less than a second and it was the longest second of the night. Spider saw it for what it was: the rest of them are predators of people, and I am a predator of them. He didn't flinch. *You're good,* he said, *and that keeps me good,* and we let the door close over it because there was a pickup to plan.[^r3]

You've known this about me more plainly than I've let myself. I don't yet know what it costs to be the thing in the room the other monsters keep an eye on, and when I find out, you'll be the one I tell. The boxes land tomorrow night and we hold the guests through the day, so I'll be later than usual finding you — here, in the borrowed dark, being good because someone needs me to be. Tell the girls it's a work trip. It's even true.[^r4]
