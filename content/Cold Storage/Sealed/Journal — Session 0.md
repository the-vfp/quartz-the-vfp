You had a knife when I got home. You'll say it isn't fair of me to begin there. I don't have a fair place to begin tonight.

You weren't using it. You weren't even holding it the way a person holds a knife — the santoku, the good one, the one you keep sharp because the cooking is yours and you are vain about it in the only domestic way you let yourself be vain — it was just lying open in your hand while you sat on the edge of the bed with your phone burning in the other, and you were somewhere I couldn't get to. I said your name three times. The first two did nothing, and I have spent a professional life among quiet rooms and never once been frightened of one until the two seconds between the second time I said your name and the third.[^r1]

Then you came back — brisk, already reaching for the laptop, as though you'd only stepped out to take a call — which is the thing about you I've never been able to decide whether to be grateful for, because the speed of your recovery lets me pretend I didn't see the place you'd gone. I saw it.

The girls are asleep. We checked together. That's the only part of tonight I don't have to think about.

The rest I do. A voicemail on your personal cell, a voice changer laid over a woman's cadence, and I made myself listen twice instead of once, which you'd have told me not to do — the phrasing was rehearsed, not read off a page, which means she's said it before or stood somewhere private and practiced, and I mind the practicing more than the threat. The demand isn't a lump sum, it's a standing order: a quarter of [[Bridgepoint Consulting|Bridgepoint]] every month, a tap and not a robbery, which means she intends to be in our lives a long time and has done the arithmetic to be sure we can survive letting her. The leverage is a video of me feeding — a 7-Eleven bathroom, five weeks ago, a phone and not a ceiling camera, which means a person, close, on purpose.[^r2] And all of it came to your work address, the one that isn't anywhere public, the one it took effort and intent to find, and I keep going back to that the way the tongue goes back to a broken tooth.

You ran the routing numbers at the kitchen table while I made calls, and the trail goes to something small and too tidy called [[Darkhour Security]] — you're leaning cutout, I'm leaning nothing yet,[^rdk] and we agreed about it the way we agree about everything that frightens us, which is by not arguing. The contingency wire is prepped. There's a knot of [[Camarilla]] neonates already worrying at Darkhour for reasons of their own I don't have and don't like not having, but a door is a door, and it is easier to walk beside people already pointed the right way than to start cold and blunder across their work. You keep the money. I'll bring you what the neonates spill. We'll compare notes the way we always do, which is to say badly, at four in the morning, each of us pretending not to count who is more afraid.

The plan is live; you take the girls out tomorrow night. I won't write down where, not here, not anywhere — you taught me that, years ago, the first time, and I resented you for it then the way one resents people who turn out to have been right.

We haven't talked about the gas station. We won't tonight; we never do it on the night. I remember it, though. I was hungry and careless and there was nothing in it I would ever have chosen for you to see, and now you've seen it, in someone else's framing, at a moment of someone else's choosing, and you didn't say a word — you went from the knife on the bed straight to the routing numbers, and I let you, partly because we needed them and partly because I am a coward in exactly one room of my life and it is the one with you in it.

She went to your phone. Your email. Not mine. She went around me, deliberately, to the one part of my life I can't stand guard over the way I guard the rest, and that wasn't convenience, it was a choice — and you said it before I could, your eyes still on the screen: she's been watching a while.[^r3]
