You're asleep down the hall. I'm writing to you anyway, the way I wrote on the nights I couldn't reach you at all, because the habit set into me harder than I expected, and because there's a version of a night that only exists once I've put it to you and watched you not flinch. Indulge me. Or read it over coffee and tell me where I'm wrong, which is the same thing.[^r1]

The work has changed shape. We aren't running for our lives anymore; we've been handed a post. The coterie is a watchman crew for the [[Camarilla]] now, formally — eyes on whatever threatens the Kindred of this city — and it is, to my own ongoing surprise, nearer my actual profession than anything I've done since the night I died. Less feeding in gas-station bathrooms. More sitting in a parked car with an org chart, which I'll admit only to you that I've missed.

It came the way these things apparently come now: an index card under each of our doors in the same hand, an address and the words *right now*, signed by [[Damian]], the Sheriff, who met us in a fast-food booth with an untouched burger and the posture of a man sent on an errand he'd never have chosen. The envelope he left is the Prince's, in a cipher I'll spare you — two important Kindred arrive tomorrow night, we collect them, we keep them somewhere safe for a day, we deliver them rested and whole the night after. And three local difficulties to quiet first, subtly, *no stains on the ground*, before they can hand the anarchs a grievance.

Three names, and they're a research problem before they're anything else, which is the one part of this I'm sure I'm good at. The first calls himself Fly Boy — [[Noah Grewal]] — who claims the airport and runs his grip on it through a mortal at the Department of Aviation, [[Felicity Miller]]; I mapped the hierarchy and she's the hinge, and if I can lift her off the board without blood it's clean work, though "influence" in a coded letter could mean a leash no org chart would show me.[^r2] The second is Crook — [[Charles Dawson]] — a Nosferatu running the original [[Crooks Lounge]], old enough to be bored, which makes him dangerous the way the bored ones are and not the violent ones; we struck a deal I don't love, five debts in a ledger I can't audit against a man whose whole clan specializes in not being seen, and I signed it because the alternative was worse. The third is [[Gengis]], the face of the anarchs, and the dossier calls him a Tremere puppet, which is bait — [[Trip]]'s sire walked us through it: there's a splinter, [[House Carna]], and a woman, [[Erika]], who seeds rumors to make arrogant people overreach, and the puppet story is hers. Gengis is a rich, ugly brute who wasn't home. We found a matchbox in his kitchen, a bar called Mohawk, and it's the only real thread I have.

We were chosen for this because no one knows who we are. That's the whole of our usefulness to the Prince — we're deniable — and Crook worked it out in about a minute and thought it was funny. I don't. I keep turning over how little distance there is between *useful because no one knows us* and *expendable because no one knows us*, and I'd rather we were watching for the night that distance closes than reading about it afterward in someone's handwriting.[^r3]

But the girls are upstairs and you're down the hall, and the worst thing in my life tonight is a bored Nosferatu's ledger and a matchbook. After the spring we had, I'll take it. I'll be up before you. Don't let me sleep past the girls' breakfast.[^r4]
