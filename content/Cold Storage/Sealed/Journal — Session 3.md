I found your name tonight. Handwritten on a sticky note, pinned under a months-old photo of [[Carter]] on that wall in the [[Darkhour Office|Darkhour]] office.

Raphael Marte. Carter Marshall. [[Megan]]. [[Gladys Ackerman]]. [[Jonathan]]. Five names, and the moment we read them in a row the whole thing held still long enough to show what it was. None of us was chosen at random; every one of us was leaned on through someone we love — and [[Collin]], who hadn't been approached at all, which had bothered me for two nights without my being able to say why, is on there too. His name is waiting because they hadn't taken Jonathan yet. They were saving him.[^r1]

Someone built a catalogue of the people each of us would do anything to keep, and then worked it the way I'd work a catalogue, methodically, in order. I called you the second I could. You told me nothing had changed on your end and I believed you, and I drove the rest of the night anyway with your name in a stranger's handwriting sitting behind my eyes where I couldn't put it down.[^r2]

We took the gate runner apart first, gently. He came up sterile — no wallet, no card, nothing a person carries — which is its own piece of information, because someone walked him into the night clean on purpose. I read his blood before we spoke: melancholic, thin, fleeting. I fed off him, lightly, clean against my convictions; a man who stalked Carter for a paycheck is not owed my restraint. You'd appreciate the method of it more than the others do — I don't perform anything, I read a man the way I'd read a record, and the blood is only a source that doesn't get to edit itself. He gave a name eventually, Jim, and very little after it. The outfit takes gig work; their client is a handle, [[Redwood]], and only Jensen ever met him. We tried Jensen's burner and the line was already dead. Whoever Redwood is, he is careful, and he is not improvising.

Collin was the part I wasn't ready for. His phone rang in the middle of all of it — the kidnapper, distorted, *I have your special friend, tick tock* — and he came apart, put his fist through the phone and then the workbench. [[Spider]] and [[Mitch]] pinned his arms and I put my face in his and told him to get himself together, that we don't find anyone if he loses it, that we need him — not gently, because he doesn't need gentle, he needs someone to hold still while everything else moves, and tonight that was me.[^r3]

The trail ran through [[Garfield Park]], which is Elysium, neutral ground, a thing I didn't know until Trip and Spider did. Before we knew it, Trip had his eye on someone sleeping rough, and I said no; I didn't take it back once the ground made it permissible, because permissible and right aren't shelved in the same section. I kept my senses up and my mouth shut, and I watched.

It ends at a house that doesn't belong to its street — Greek statues in the yard, a cartoon Hercules set at the door like a joke left for whoever's clever enough to take it — and two dogs that came at the fence too fast and too large, and Mitch caught Trip's arm before he could step through the gate. *Those aren't dogs.* He's right. They're ghouls, which means the man who has been taking the people we love keeps a standing supply of our blood, and is not, himself, a man.[^r4]

We go through the gate tomorrow night. Jonathan is in there, and so is whatever made those dogs.

Give the girls my love. I'll find you before I sleep.
