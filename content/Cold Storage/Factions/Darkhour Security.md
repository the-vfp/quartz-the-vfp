# Darkhour Security

## Profile

On paper, a local security company. In practice, a shell — a bare website, a phone that goes to voicemail, online security tight enough that [[Collin]]'s people bounced off it. The [[Darkhour Office]] in Ravenswood was freshly inhabited, mid-renovation, lived in maybe a couple of weeks, though the company had existed on paper longer.

Raphael's banking trail led us here at the start. I know now what it was: Darkhour was hired, not the hand behind it. [[Redwood|Paul Sterry Sr.]] bought them the building and ran them as a cell — they were the cutout; he was the principal.

## Known Members

- **[[Steve Jensen]]** — dead, drained by [[Spider]] at the office. The only operative who ever met the client in person.
- **The Charger driver** — female, late 20s, Eastern European features, hair up, wide-eyed and not a cool head. Drove a black Charger on a corporate fleet plate; got away. The plate is trackable.
- **The stairwell shooter** — unnamed, shot twice by [[Collin]], stabilized by [[Trip]]. Out cold for days.
- **The gate runner** — [[Jim]]. Knocked himself out on the fence; we questioned him and let him go.
- **The voicemail caller** — female, voice-changed, delivered the rehearsed tribute demand on Raphael's cell. By voice, a different person from the Charger driver.
- **The crawlspace lady** — unnamed, mortal (up in daylight). Planted the camera in [[Carter's Apartment]]; on the footage she came out of a wall, looked like she didn't know what she'd done, and went back in.
- **The blonde man** — unnamed; big, blonde, neck tattoos, hoodie. Beat [[Carter]] to find where [[Mitch]] sleeps. Description overlaps [[Steve Jensen]]'s; I never confirmed they're the same man.

## Notes

Darkhour produced the footage of [[Walter Nash]] being attacked, and at some point had been looking for [[Spider]] — a thread none of us ever tied off. They ran the whole stalker operation on [[Carter]] out of the office.

Three figures sat in a parked car watching the place the night we hit it. Never engaged, never identified.

By voice and manner, at least two different operators ran Darkhour — the voice-changed caller and the Charger driver weren't the same woman. More than one hand on the wheel.
